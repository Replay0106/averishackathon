● how it works

From a folder of shipping PDFs
to a bank-ready decision — in four stages.

NavisAI reads a shipment's paper trail, cross-checks every figure against itself, checks it against international trade law, and hands operations a ready-to-send fix — all before a discrepancy becomes a bank rejection or a demurrage fee.

Input: Invoice · Packing List · B/L · Letter of Credit Output: risk score + $ exposure + ready-to-send corrections Runtime: ~4 seconds
the pipeline

Four stages, one continuous run

Each stage takes the previous stage's output as its input. Nothing is re-entered by hand — the shipment reference flows straight through from ingestion to dispatch.

1
Ingestion & Adaptive Audit
reads the documents
IN4 PDFs — Invoice, Packing List, B/L, LC
→
OUTStructured fields + a cross-doc audit

Each PDF is read and classified: routine paperwork (invoice, packing list) goes to a fast extraction pass; dense legal text (LC clauses, origin rules) goes to an extended-reasoning pass. A deterministic checker then compares every shared field — container IDs, weights, HS codes, ports — across all four documents at once.

Adaptive Model Router ~60% compute saved risk score · $ exposure
2
Visual Redline Diff
shows what's wrong
INEvery mismatch found in Stage 1
→
OUTSide-by-side correction, ready to apply

For each mismatch, NavisAI lines up the two conflicting values — the wrong one struck through in red, the verified fix highlighted in green — the same way a lawyer redlines a contract. One toggle applies the fix to the working document set in memory, no re-upload needed.

e.g. MSCU9021432 → MSCU9021431 e.g. 51,900 kg → 52,400 kg
3
Cross-Border Statutory Gate
checks it's legal
INCorrected document set
→
OUTPass / fail per regulatory framework

Independent of whether the numbers match, NavisAI checks the shipment against the rules a bank or customs officer would actually cite: ICC UCP 600 Articles 14 & 18 (document examination and invoice value), MITI / ATIGA Form D rules of origin, and destination customs standards such as Port of Rotterdam clearance rules.

UCP 600 Art. 14 UCP 600 Art. 18 ATIGA Form D
4
Navis Dispatch Engine
acts on it
INEvery unresolved issue, tagged by type
→
OUTDrafted message, queued and dispatched

Every remaining issue is routed to the party who can actually fix it — a carrier amendment email to the shipping line, a trade-finance alert to the bank desk, or a customs broker inquiry — each pre-drafted and queued with a recipient and a timestamp, ready for one-click send.

context

Who uses this, and on what

Who's looking at this screen

A trade-operations auditor at a shared-services desk, working through a queue of shipments before documents go to the bank for Letter of Credit presentation.

  • They don't want to hunt for a typo across four PDFs by eye.
  • They need to know which law a mismatch breaks, not just that it exists.
  • They need something they can send — not just a red flag.

What's actually being read

Real shipment paperwork for commodity exports — palm oil, pulp & paper — the kind where a single container ID or weight figure has to match, word for word, across every document in the set.

  • Commercial Invoice — declared value, HS code, container
  • Packing List — gross / net weight, container
  • Bill of Lading — carrier's record of the same fields
  • Letter of Credit — the bank's binding conditions
